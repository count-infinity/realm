# 065. Pet

> Checklist item 65 ([now]): *db.following, listen-lock whitelists, spoken orders*

**What you'll build:** Biscuit, a ginger hound. Say "Biscuit, heel" and he
pads after you through every exit you take; "stay" parks him where he stands;
"speak" gets a bark. Only you, his owner, can give the orders, and that rule is
one lock on his ears rather than an if-check in every script.

**Concepts:** the engine's follow system driven by one attribute (`following`),
`^listen` triggers as a voice-command surface, the listen lock as an order
whitelist, and [`set_attr`](../reference/softcode.md#fn-set_attr) /
[`del_attr`](../reference/softcode.md#fn-del_attr) writing that attribute from
softcode.

## How it works

The finished pet is an NPC with three overheard words and one lock. There is no
leash code and no movement loop: heel writes a single attribute and the engine's
own follow system carries Biscuit through every exit you take, stay deletes that
attribute, and a lock on his hearing keeps the orders yours alone. This section
answers three questions: how one attribute makes him follow, why the orders are
just speech, and how the lock keeps strangers out.

### How one attribute makes him follow

REALM's whole follow system is the `following` attribute: a follower stores its
leader's id there, and when that leader walks through an exit, everything in the
room whose `following` names them walks after. The cascade is engine-driven,
because `move_through_exit` calls `bring_followers` once the leader has
relocated, and each follower takes the *real* exit, so locks, closed doors, and
guard behaviors apply to it exactly as they would to you. Only unconscious or
in-combat followers stay behind. Players opt in with the `follow` command, and
anything else opts in by writing the attribute, which is all a pet does. Because
the state is one ordinary attribute, `party` lists Biscuit in your group and
`@examine Biscuit` shows `following` when he is yours to walk and nothing once
he is told to stay.

### Why the orders are just speech

Each trick is a `^listen` trigger (`^*heel*`, `^*stay*`, `^*speak*`), so an
order is nothing more than talking near him: "Biscuit, heel", "HEEL, damn you",
and "would you heel" all match. A `^listen` trigger fires only on the object
that carries it, and never on the speaker themselves, so Biscuit reacts to your
words with no room-wide broadcast to filter. Each trick runs as Biscuit and
mutates only Biscuit (his `following`, his poses), which means a shouted order
can never reach anything but the dog.

### How the lock keeps strangers out

Anyone standing in the room can say "heel", so the whitelist has to be a policy,
not a check inside each script. The engine gates whose speech may fire an
object's `^listen` triggers with the listen lock, and one line states the
policy:

```text
@lock/listen Biscuit = caller.id == owner.id
```

In a listen lock, `caller` is the speaker and `owner` is Biscuit's owner, which
is you, since you `@create`d him. Every `^pattern` on Biscuit now ignores
everyone else, the current tricks and any you add later alike. (Compare
[the bartender](064_bartender.md), whose listen triggers are deliberately
public.) One dog, one line of policy, and zero per-trick checks.

## Build it

Dig a yard off your workroom and make the dog. `@create` puts Biscuit in your
hands, and `drop` sets him loose on the ground:

```text
@dig The Kennel Yard = yard, out
yard
@create Biscuit
@tag Biscuit = npc
drop Biscuit
@desc Biscuit = A ginger hound with an opinion about everything.
```

The listen lock is the entire ownership policy. It gates every `^listen`
trigger on Biscuit, so only your speech reaches his ears:

```text
@lock/listen Biscuit = caller.id == owner.id
```

Heel is a `^listen` trigger that writes his `following` attribute and then poses,
naming the owner with [`name`](../reference/softcode.md#fn-name). The two
statements make it a `'''` block (see
[multi-line input](../guides/world-management.md#multi-line-input-heredocs)),
with the `^*heel*:` pattern on its own first line:

```text
@set Biscuit/listen_heel = '''
^*heel*:
set_attr(me, 'following', enactor.id)  # this one attribute is the whole follow system
pose(f"falls in at {name(enactor)}'s heel.")
'''
```

Stay is the inverse: it deletes the attribute, so the engine stops walking him
after you:

```text
@set Biscuit/listen_stay = '''
^*stay*:
del_attr(me, 'following')  # removing it stops him following
pose('sits, ears up, exactly where told.')
'''
```

Speak changes no state, so it stays a one-line trigger that only poses:

```text
@set Biscuit/listen_speak = ^*speak*:pose('barks once, sharp and proud.')
```

That is the entire pet: three overheard words and a lock.

## Try it

```text
say Biscuit, speak      -> Biscuit barks once, sharp and proud.
say Biscuit, heel       -> Biscuit falls in at Tam's heel.
out                     -> you step out and Biscuit follows through the
                           real exit; walk anywhere (stairs, gates,
                           guarded doors) and what blocks you blocks him
party                   -> Biscuit — following Tam
```

Now the whitelist. Have a friend try to poach him:

```text
(Rook) say Biscuit, stay   -> nothing; not a twitch
(you)  say Biscuit, stay   -> Biscuit sits, ears up, exactly where told.
out                        -> you leave; Biscuit stays put
```

`@examine Biscuit` at any time shows `following` while he is walking with you
and nothing once he is told to stay, which is the whole system in one attribute.

## Going further

- **Fetch:** `^*fetch*:cmd('get ' + arg0)` routes the scripted `get` through the
  real verb, so he cannot fetch what is locked down. The bound wildcard (`arg0`)
  carries what was said after the trigger word.
- **Feeding and mood:** add an `ON_RECEIVE` hook (borrow
  [the aggressive mob](062_aggressive_mob.md)'s offering pattern) that calls
  [`adjust_disposition`](../reference/softcode.md#fn-adjust_disposition)`(me,
  enactor, 1)`, then gate the tricks on
  [`disposition`](../reference/softcode.md#fn-disposition)`(me, enactor)` so a
  neglected dog stops listening. An `ON_RECEIVE` hook fires on every object in
  the room, so guard its body with
  [`if target is me:`](../reference/softcode.md#guard-on-target); the `^listen`
  tricks need no such guard, because a listen trigger only ever fires on the
  object that owns it.
- **A guard dog:** compose with [the aggressive mob](062_aggressive_mob.md):
  `@behavior Biscuit = aggressive, target_tags:["burglar"]` makes him a pet and
  a defense at once, with `spare_at` sparing the family.
- **Transferable leash:** replace the lock with
  `caller.id == get_attr(target, 'trusted', '')` and add a `$entrust *` command
  that writes the `trusted` attribute with
  [`set_attr`](../reference/softcode.md#fn-set_attr), giving you a pet you can
  lend. ([`get_attr`](../reference/softcode.md#fn-get_attr) reads it back on
  every order.)
