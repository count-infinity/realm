# 065. Pet

> Checklist item 65 ([now]): *a `companions` bond from softcode, listen-lock whitelists, spoken tricks, `order`*

**What you'll build:** Biscuit, a ginger hound. Say "Biscuit, heel" and he
becomes your companion: he pads after you through every exit you take,
obeys `order`, and fights at your side. "Stay" parks him where he stands
without making him any less yours; "speak" gets a bark. Only you, his
owner, can give the spoken orders, and that rule is one lock on his ears
rather than an if-check in every script.

**Concepts:** the engine's [companions](../guides/companions.md) (a
`master` attribute that brings following, `order` and fighting together
with it), [`set_master`](../reference/softcode.md#fn-set_master) bonding
from softcode, `^listen` triggers as a voice-command surface, and the
listen lock as an order whitelist.

## How it works

The finished pet is an NPC with three overheard words and one lock. There is
no leash code and no movement loop: heel makes him your companion with one
softcode call, stay deletes his `following` attribute, and a lock on his
hearing keeps the spoken orders yours alone. This section answers three
questions: what being your companion gives him, why the tricks are just
speech, and how the lock keeps strangers out.

### What does being your companion give him?

A companion is an NPC whose `master` attribute names you; the `companions`
feature (MUD_PLAN step 13) does the rest.
[`set_master`](../reference/softcode.md#fn-set_master)`(me, enactor)` sets
`master`, and with it the `following` attribute, so when you walk through
an exit, the `social` feature's follow system walks him after you through
the *real* exit: locks, closed doors and guard behaviors apply to him
exactly as they would to you. Being your companion also means:

- `order Biscuit <command>` runs the command as him, through the real
  dispatcher (admin commands, `order` and `quit` are refused);
- when you attack or are attacked, he joins the fight on your side, and
  what he kills is credited to you;
- he never turns on you, and he leaves and comes back with you when you
  log out and in;
- `companions` lists him, and he counts against how many followers you may
  keep (two, unless the game says otherwise).

Stay only deletes `following`: he stops walking after you but is still
yours, so `order Biscuit follow Tam` (or "heel" again) brings him along.

### Why are the tricks just speech?

Each trick is a `^listen` trigger (`^*heel*`, `^*stay*`, `^*speak*`), so an
order is nothing more than talking near him: "Biscuit, heel", "HEEL, damn
you", and "would you heel" all match. A `^listen` trigger fires only on the
object that carries it, and never on the speaker themselves, so Biscuit
reacts to your words with no room-wide broadcast to filter. Each trick runs
as Biscuit and changes only Biscuit (his bond, his `following`, his poses),
which means a shouted order can never reach anything but the dog.

### How does the lock keep strangers out?

Anyone standing in the room can say "heel", so the whitelist has to be a policy,
not a check inside each script. The engine gates whose speech may fire an
object's `^listen` triggers with the listen lock, and one line states the
policy:

```text
@lock/listen Biscuit = caller.id == owner.id
```

In a listen lock, `caller` is the speaker and `owner` is Biscuit's owner, which
is you, since you `@create`d him. Every `^pattern` on Biscuit now ignores
everyone else, the current tricks and any you add later alike. (Compare
[the bartender](064_bartender.md), whose listen triggers are deliberately
public.) `order` needs no lock: it obeys only his master.

## Build it

Dig a yard off your workroom and make the dog. `@create` puts Biscuit in your
hands, and `drop` sets him loose on the ground:

```text
@dig The Kennel Yard = yard, out
yard
@create Biscuit
@tag Biscuit = npc
drop Biscuit
@desc Biscuit = A ginger hound with an opinion about everything.
```

The listen lock is the entire ownership policy. It gates every `^listen`
trigger on Biscuit, so only your speech reaches his ears:

```text
@lock/listen Biscuit = caller.id == owner.id
```

Heel is a `^listen` trigger that bonds him to the speaker (the first time)
and puts him back at your heel (every time), then poses, naming you with
[`name`](../reference/softcode.md#fn-name). The statements make it a `'''`
block (see
[multi-line input](../guides/world-management.md#multi-line-input-heredocs)),
with the `^*heel*:` pattern on its own first line:

```text
@set Biscuit/listen_heel = '''
^*heel*:
set_master(me, enactor)                    # he is yours: follows, obeys order, fights beside you
set_attr(me, 'following', enactor.id)      # back at heel after a stay
pose(f"falls in at {name(enactor)}'s heel.")
'''
```

Stay deletes `following` only, so he stops walking after you but stays
yours:

```text
@set Biscuit/listen_stay = '''
^*stay*:
del_attr(me, 'following')  # parked, still your companion
pose('sits, ears up, exactly where told.')
'''
```

Speak changes no state, so it stays a one-line trigger that only poses:

```text
@set Biscuit/listen_speak = ^*speak*:pose('barks once, sharp and proud.')
```

That is the entire pet: three overheard words and a lock.

## Try it

```text
say Biscuit, speak      -> Biscuit barks once, sharp and proud.
say Biscuit, heel       -> Biscuit falls in at Tam's heel.
companions              -> Biscuit   here
                           You have 1 of 2 followers.
order Biscuit pose rolls over.
                        -> You order Biscuit to 'pose rolls over.'
                           Biscuit rolls over.
out                     -> you step out and Biscuit follows through the
                           real exit; walk anywhere (stairs, gates,
                           guarded doors) and what blocks you blocks him
party                   -> Biscuit — following Tam
```

Now the whitelist. Have a friend try to poach him:

```text
(Rook) say Biscuit, stay   -> nothing; not a twitch
(Rook) order Biscuit sit   -> Do it yourself!
(you)  say Biscuit, stay   -> Biscuit sits, ears up, exactly where told.
out                        -> you leave; Biscuit stays put
companions                 -> Biscuit   in The Kennel Yard
```

`@examine Biscuit` shows `master` (yours for good) and `following` while
he is walking with you.

## Going further

- **Fetch:** `^*fetch*:cmd('get ' + arg0)` routes the scripted `get` through the
  real verb, so he cannot fetch what is locked down; `order Biscuit get ball`
  does the same without a trigger. The bound wildcard (`arg0`) carries what was
  said after the trigger word.
- **Feeding and mood:** add an `ON_RECEIVE` hook (borrow
  [the aggressive mob](062_aggressive_mob.md)'s offering pattern) that calls
  [`adjust_disposition`](../reference/softcode.md#fn-adjust_disposition)`(me,
  enactor, 1)`, then gate the tricks on
  [`disposition`](../reference/softcode.md#fn-disposition)`(me, enactor)` so a
  neglected dog stops listening. An `ON_RECEIVE` hook fires on every object in
  the room, so guard its body with
  [`if target is me:`](../reference/softcode.md#guard-on-target); the `^listen`
  tricks need no such guard, because a listen trigger only ever fires on the
  object that owns it.
- **A guard dog:** a companion fights beside its master without being told;
  for a dog that also bites strangers, compose with
  [the aggressive mob](062_aggressive_mob.md) on a dog that is *not* a
  companion (`@behavior Biscuit = aggressive, target_tags:["burglar"]`): a
  companion never attacks on sight.
- **Let him go:** `dismiss Biscuit`, or a `^*shoo*` trick calling
  [`release`](../reference/softcode.md#fn-release)`(me)`; his `ON_RELEASE`
  hook can sulk.
- **A pet shop:** sell copies of him: a shopkeeper with `sells_pets` and a
  `pet_stock` naming him ([the guide](../guides/companions.md#pet-shops)).
